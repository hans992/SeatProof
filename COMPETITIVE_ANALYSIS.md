## 1. MSP export reconciliation — selected hypothesis

**Competitors/substitutes:** Gradient Reconcile, CloudOlive, HaloPSA configuration, distributor sync, Excel/Power Query and an outsourced bookkeeper. Gradient advertises $299/month for up to 150 active-contract customers, higher tiers and a $199 Microsoft add-on (S04). It synchronizes usage and supports approval/write-back (its [pricing help](https://support.meetgradient.com/billable-pro-pricing)). CloudOlive explicitly reconciles nonintegrated vendor information (S05), with bundles, invoices and PSA workflows described in vendor materials. Current numeric CloudOlive pricing was not verified: the production page returned no extractable text; a staging page is not a quote. Halo recurring subscription capabilities exist, and a practitioner says setup solved billing (S02).

**Complaints and counterevidence:** S03 reports integration breakage and missing mapping notifications in February 2025. Do not assume those remain. S06 praises CloudOlive's results. S07 includes both a strong recommendation and a complaint that input files leave manual work. CSV input is therefore not novel and may be a drawback.

**Possible wedge:** optional local operation, small explicit export contract, evidence hashes, visible unresolved rows and no required PSA integration. None is demonstrated to command a separate budget or to be unavailable elsewhere. The primary differentiation experiment is purchase/onboarding preference, not a feature checklist.

**Buyer/procurement:** owner or finance lead at a 3–20-person MSP; proposed 200–2,000 supported seats. The employee and seat bands are hypotheses. Existing spend supports category budget but not our €149 price. A local paid diagnostic could avoid system replacement; repeat export burden could destroy retention. Switching requires mappings, billing policies and staff trust. Channels: independent MSP bookkeepers, PSA consultants and permission-based community participation. Required input: vendor exports plus PSA charges with comparable periods and customer identifiers. No API connector in MVP. Timing: recurring fragmentation is established, not an AI trend bet. Incumbents win on integrations, support, bundle handling and billing relationships.

## 2. Purchase-order confirmation checking

**Competitors/substitutes:** SAP Business ByDesign/ERP confirmations, EDI, Conexiom and Procure Ai. SAP documents manual and automated acknowledgement receipt (S11). Conexiom's product description includes vendor acknowledgements, not just sales-order entry (S12). Numeric pricing is negotiated, not publicly verified (S13). Procure Ai markets confirmation-to-ERP automation ([public-sector workflow](https://www.procure.ai/industry/public-sector)); no numeric price verified. Canals documents quote/order entry from messages, but that should not be conflated with full buyer-side confirmation reconciliation ([help](https://hub.canals.ai/en/articles/4179713)).

**Complaints:** S09 reports mismatching price/quantity/freight, counted once despite cross-posting. S10 concerns missing acknowledgements, a related but different bottleneck. Conexiom reviews surfaced a positive aggregate; no specific current defect was independently verified. **Gap:** non-EDI small-supplier exceptions without ERP rollout is plausible but not proven. **Switching:** supplier part aliases, split deliveries, revisions and ERP permissions. **Budget:** procurement/operations has labor budget, but no SME quote or purchase commitment obtained. **Distribution:** ERP implementation partners and specialist purchasing networks; founder has no established channel. **Integration:** read PO export, parse PDFs/emails, reconcile units and delivery schedules. **Timing:** unstructured supplier communication persists; AI can parse variety. **Incumbent advantage:** mapping libraries and ERP integration. Hold for interviews; not a defensible generic document product.

## 3. Freight invoice evidence review

**Competitors/substitutes:** Loop, Shipware, managed freight audit services, TMS rules and internal AP. Loop markets document/rate audits and contract digitization (S16). No numeric Loop price located. Shipware describes shared-savings fees with no monthly retainers for specified audit services; percentage unknown (S17). A subscription would have to outperform that risk-sharing proposition.

**Evidence/complaints:** GSA and ECTA establish concrete audit workflows (S14–S15); they do not establish SME dissatisfaction. A founder's claimed twenty-team interview survey was excluded as independent evidence. No reliably matched current Loop freight review complaint was obtained; Loop Returns is a different product. **Gap hypothesis:** evidence packet for small accessorial disputes. **Switching:** carrier relationships and contracts, tariffs, shipment identifiers and historic dispute outcomes. **Budget/procurement:** transport finance controls freight spend but may outsource recovery. **Channels:** freight consultants/accountants, shipper associations; no established reach. **Integration:** invoice, rate card, POD, timestamps, accessorial rules and currency. **Timing:** stable complexity; no speculative regulatory urgency asserted. **Incumbent advantage:** rate engines, recovery teams and contingency fees. Too much onboarding for the initial build.

## 4. Retail deduction recovery

**Competitors/substitutes:** SPS Commerce Revenue Recovery/SupplyPike, retailer portals, AR staff and recovery agencies. Vendor help explains retailer-specific codes and export structure (S20). Software Advice lists custom pricing (S19), not a numeric quote. **Complaints:** no reliable individual complaint retained; do not manufacture one from generic vendor descriptions. Strong aggregate reviews are counterevidence. **Missing workflow hypothesis:** smaller brands lacking evidence collection; already central to incumbent positioning. **Switching:** retailer credentials, historical deductions, remittances, PODs, mapping and submission procedures. **Budget:** recoverable money creates a budget argument but recovery and disputability are uncertain. **Channels:** CPG finance consultants and retailer supplier communities. **Integration:** portals, EDI, warehouse and accounting. **Timing:** recurring established deductions, not a newly discovered gap. **Incumbent advantage:** retailer knowledge and installed integrations. Reject for founder constraints.

## 5. Supplier catalog normalization

**Competitors/substitutes:** Akeneo SDM, Plytix PIM, ETL scripts, spreadsheets and agency services. Akeneo explicitly implements typing, required attributes, transformations, error review and supplier collaboration (S21–S22). No numeric SDM price verified. Plytix's dynamic pricing rendered unusable base values; no zero-price claim is made (S23). **Complaints:** G2 category summaries discuss setup and reporting difficulties (S24); these are mediated summaries, not fresh customer interviews. **Gap hypothesis:** smaller one-off supplier imports, but the same workflow already exists. **Switching:** taxonomy, assets, supplier portals and storefront mappings. **Budget/procurement:** merchandising/data teams spend on PIM, often with multiple stakeholders. **Channels:** ecommerce/PIM agencies. **Integration:** PIM, supplier files and channel-specific schemas. **Timing:** persistent data-quality problem; generative enrichment adds error risks. **Incumbent advantage:** taxonomy, distribution endpoints and installed workflows. Reject generic version.

Reject generic entrant.

## 7. Agency scope leakage

**Competitors/substitutes:** Productive, project budgets, written change orders, contracts and PM judgment. Productive advertises budgeting and related agency operations; its retrieved Essential price widget is internally inconsistent, so no normalized numeric comparison is used (S30). **Complaints:** S28 and S29 describe unpaid changes despite visible scope evidence. These do not prove demand for automated detection. **Gap hypothesis:** message-to-scope comparison at the moment of request; easy to copy and may not resolve reluctance to confront a client. **Switching:** contracts, project records and communication access. **Budget:** PM tools and labor exist, but no new-product willingness-to-pay evidence. **Channels:** agency owner groups and fractional operations leaders. **Integration:** scope text, messages and budget/rate data. **Timing:** more AI-assisted requests could worsen expectations but is speculative. **Incumbent advantage:** workflow ownership. Reject due to behavioral bottleneck.
