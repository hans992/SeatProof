# Customer discovery protocol

> Historical record — superseded on 28 October 2026 by [COMMERCIAL_VERDICT.md](COMMERCIAL_VERDICT.md). Commercial development is stopped; no compelling advantage over the four compared products was established. Earlier pilot, pricing, outreach and feature proposals below are inactive hypotheses, not the current recommendation.

Goal: disprove the proposed workflow and price as quickly as possible. The interviewer must not treat politeness, interest in AI, a demo compliment or a request for free software as purchase evidence.

## Thirty-minute conversation

1. **Context, 3 minutes:** What services do you bill recurrently? Who prepares and approves invoices? Which PSA, suppliers and billing bases are involved?
2. **Last event, 7 minutes:** Tell me about the last time a vendor count and customer charge did not agree. When was it? How was it found? What happened next? Ask for the actual sequence, not a generic estimate.
3. **Current workflow, 7 minutes:** Walk through the last monthly review. What do you export, map, check and approve? What is automatically handled by Halo/Gradient/CloudOlive or a distributor? What remains manual? What was the setup effort?
4. **Cost and consequence, 4 minutes:** What time or paid labor did that specific review consume? Was an invoice actually changed, money recovered or a dispute avoided? Can that be verified? Separate potential exposure from realized outcome.
5. **Constraints, 4 minutes:** Which data can you legitimately export? Can both sides use stable customer/product identifiers? How frequent are bundles, minimums, proration and currency differences? What would prevent using a local app?
6. **Alternatives and purchase, 5 minutes:** Why not fix the current setup? Who can approve a small diagnostic purchase? After describing the exact scope and limits, offer the proposed €249 diagnostic. Ask what would need to be true to pay. A promised future budget is not a sale.

## Observe before demonstrating

Ask permission to view an anonymized workflow. Avoid collecting customer names, staff identities, secrets or live credentials. Do not record without consent. Agree retention and deletion before receiving exports. Start with synthetic data if permission is unclear. The initial prototype is not approved for sensitive production records.

## Evidence sheet

Record company identifier/site, date, participant role (not unnecessary personal details), workflow observed versus reported, last incident, documented effort, existing tooling/spend if voluntarily disclosed, source-data availability, decision-maker, willingness-to-pay action, objections, and disconfirming evidence. Mark reported values as reported; never turn them into measured results.

## Success and kill questions

- Would a separate export review create more work than configuring your current tool?
- What would make this unsuitable even if the comparison were correct?
- Which finding looks wrong and why?
- Can you produce this month's two comparable exports in under ten minutes? Measure, don't guess.
- Would you pay for this exact diagnostic now? If no, what do you use instead?
- Would you voluntarily run it again next month? Test with a second paid cycle.

Stop or materially change the hypothesis if no one buys after ten qualified offers, data preparation dominates the job, core requirements demand unsupported bundles/period logic, or existing tools solve it after modest configuration. Keep refusals in the same research record as positive feedback.
